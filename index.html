A bit about me

At RESRG Automotive I own the analytics layer of a global operations platform — 20+ plants across North America, APAC and EMEA. The stack is a modern one: Snowflake and dbt for the models, Python, SQL and Prefect for the pipelines, AWS underneath, and Power BI and Streamlit where people actually read the numbers.

The part I care most about is the end closest to the business. Twenty plants ran on manual Excel trackers, each with its own answer for the same question. The modelling was the easy half — getting those plants to agree on what a KPI means took longer than the pipeline did, and that work became a governance app and 130+ KPI definitions everyone now builds against. I also forecast plant energy consumption from production variables for the purchasing team, which meant joining utility invoices with production and financial data until the number was something you could buy on.

I came to data through biomedical engineering — an internship at UPV's Biomedical Data Science Lab and a thesis on making clinical ML models auditable. On my own time I build the same kind of pipeline at a scale I can pay for: both projects on this page are real, tested and running at €0. I'm looking for a role where the modelling decides something — a team that treats data as a product with contracts and tests, not as a reporting chore.

How I work

Understand the business first

A model is worth what the decision behind it is worth. I start from who acts on the number, and from whether the effect can be measured afterwards.

Systems, not tasks

A request is usually a symptom. Solving the process behind it costs more once and stops the request coming back every month.

Built to be maintained

The real cost of a pipeline is the years after it ships. Clear definitions, tested grain, readable lineage — so the next person does not have to guess.

Fast, without the debt

Shipping quickly and leaving a mess are not the same trade. Tests and contracts are what make the quick version safe to keep.
