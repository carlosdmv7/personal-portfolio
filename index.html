Job Market Intelligence Engine

Which open data jobs in Europe ask for what I already know?

The problem
Job boards search by title and city. Whether a role wants your tools is buried in the ad, so you open fifty ads to find five.
What it does
Every morning it reads each new posting, notes the tools it asks for, and ranks open roles by how much of that list your CV already covers.
What it found
It dropped its own first idea: across 730 postings, one said outright that it sponsors visas. And Spanish, German and Dutch ads only look less technical, because their main board publishes a teaser, not the ad.

Spanish Housing Radar

Is this flat actually cheap, or does it just have a low price?

The problem
Portals show a price, never whether it is a good one. €225,000 means nothing until you know what the flats around it ask per square metre.
What it does
Every week it collects València's listings and scores each flat against similar ones in its own neighbourhood, and says how many flats that comparison rests on.
What it found
A 120 m² flat in Patraix asking a third less per m² than its barrio. And its own worst mistake: the top “great deal” was once a 707 m² one-bedroom, a typo for 70. Errors like it are now screened out, in the open.

A bit about me

What I enjoy most is the stretch between a messy process and a tool people actually use. I start with the people who run it — what they decide, what slows them down — simplify the process first, and only then build the pipeline and the app behind it. Something small in real use this week beats something complete in a slide deck.

I come from biomedical engineering and an MSc in data analysis and process improvement, and kept one rule from there: if someone is going to act on a number, they should be able to see where it came from. I build the same way outside work — both projects on this page are live, tested and rebuilt on a schedule.
